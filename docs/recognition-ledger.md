The ledger, kept at the foot of the regulator view, answers a different question than the metrics: not "what is the chain, measurably" but "what have authorities formally said about it." Its evidentiary value rests entirely on the narrowness of its rules.

## The inclusion rule

An entry must be a formal instrument of a governmental or self-regulatory authority that names Cardano or ADA specifically: a statute, a rule or interpretive release, an order, a no-action letter, or a product listing on a regulated exchange. Speeches, press coverage, pending bills, and general crypto actions that do not name ADA do not qualify. A loose rule would turn the ledger into a press-clippings page; the narrow rule is what makes an entry mean something.

## The symmetry rule

Adverse actions are recorded under exactly the same rule as favorable ones. If an authority removed ADA from a classification, or a formal action named Cardano adversely, it would be entered with the same discipline. A recognition ledger that records only good news is marketing; symmetry is what makes it evidence.

## The text discipline

Each entry carries the instrument, its date, the issuing authority, a formal citation, a link to the primary source, and either a quotation verified character for character against the instrument's published text or a clearly labeled description. Entries state facts only: no commentary, no scoring, and no implication that recognition equals endorsement.

## Append only, including about itself

The ledger and its inclusion rule are fixed by documented operator ruling, flagged for ratification by the External Advisory Panel. A wrong entry is corrected by a new entry that says so; changing the rule would itself be a new dated ruling, and entries admitted under the old rule would remain.

## Current entries

Each entry links its primary source on the regulator view.
