The portal's regulator view organizes the same evidence under four frameworks, each labeled with its status: an administrative interpretation in force, a proposed rule, and two pieces of legislation that were not enacted. This page describes the frameworks themselves and the discipline governing how they are quoted. The interpretive release is the default view; the others are reached from the framework picker.

## The status of each framework

- SEC and CFTC joint interpretive release: administrative interpretation in force since March 2026. The default framework.
- Regulation Crypto Assets: proposed rule, File No. S7-2026-27; comments close October 20, 2026. Mapped as proposed; nothing under it is a final rule.
- House H.R. 3633, the CLARITY Act: legislation not enacted. Passed the House July 17, 2025; the Senate rejected cloture on the merged text on September 15, 2026. Retained as reference.
- The Senate merged text: legislation not enacted; never enrolled. Retained as reference.

## The SEC and CFTC joint interpretive release

Release Nos. 33-11412 and 34-105020, adopted March 17, 2026, published at 91 FR 13714. The release classifies crypto assets into five categories and names ADA among its examples of digital commodities, based on characteristics it describes: a functional crypto system, value derived from programmatic operation, and no central party holding operational, economic, or voting control. It is an agency interpretation, not a statute: it governs how the agencies administer the law, does not bind courts, and speaks as of its date. The portal maps its evidence to the release's operative concepts so a reader can check, on any later date, whether the facts behind the classification still hold. The release's three categories of control, operational, economic, and voting, are also the headings under which the overview groups its measurements.

## The Regulation Crypto Assets proposal

Proposed by the SEC in a release dated August 18, 2026, published in the Federal Register on August 21, 2026 (Release Nos. 33-11434; 34-106150; File No. S7-2026-27; 91 FR 54510), a tailored offering regime for investment contracts involving crypto assets: a startup exemption of up to $5 million over four years, a fundraising exemption of up to $75 million in each twelve month period with ongoing reporting, and an investment contract safe harbor, proposed Rule 400. The safe harbor would codify the joint interpretation's separation doctrine as a rule: it applies when the issuer "has completed or otherwise permanently ceased all essential managerial efforts that it represented or promised it would engage in under the covered investment contract" and neither makes nor intends new such promises, perfected by filing a transition report, Form TR, containing a certification and a supporting analysis. The proposal adds no network-level maturity criteria: no ownership threshold, no decentralization test, and no required network measurements. Its request for comment asks, among other things, whether a more objective standard should replace the issuer-defined one.

Comments close October 20, 2026. The portal maps four of the proposal's items as a framework labeled proposed: the Rule 400(a) supporting analysis, the Rule 103(b)(7) item on methods to verify transaction history, current reporting of network events on Form 1-UC, and the request for comment on holders of record. Every text under that framework is a paraphrase except the Rule 103(b)(7) phrase quoted verbatim. If the rule is adopted, this page and the mapping will be updated on the same discipline as every other framework change; if it is adopted in a different form, the mapping changes to match.

## House H.R. 3633, the CLARITY Act

Passed by the US House of Representatives on July 17, 2025. Its Section 205 defines the conditions under which a blockchain system may be certified as a mature blockchain system, including that it is not controlled by any person or group of persons under common control, that it operates programmatically with a verifiable history, and that ownership concentration sits below a statutory threshold, quoted in the mapping as "beneficially owning, in the aggregate, twenty percent or more of the digital commodity supply." On September 15, 2026 the Senate rejected cloture on the merged market-structure text by 49 votes to 50, which ended the bill's path for 2026. The portal retains the House mapping as reference: the concepts may return in a later Congress, and the mapping shows how the same evidence would read under them. It is no longer the default view and it is no longer described as a contingency mapping.

## The Senate merged text

The Senate's market structure counterpart, combining committee versions, was never enrolled; cloture on it failed on September 15, 2026. Every provision the portal maps under this framework is a paraphrase, described from public reporting and labeled as such. No language under this framework appears in quotation marks, because only an enrolled bill could sustain that discipline. Retained as reference for the same reason as the House text.

## The citation discipline

Quoted language on the portal reproduces the cited instrument's published text exactly, verified against the source before entry, and everything else is labeled paraphrase. The distinction is shown on every provision panel. Composing statutory-sounding language and marking it verbatim would falsify the record, so the rule is enforced with the same rigor as the numbers.

## Commitments for legislative events

Adopted with the ratified design: within ten days of a bill's passage, the portal confirms its statutory mapping against the enacted text and publishes the confirmation; within thirty days of signature, the mapping is refactored to the enacted structure, which becomes the primary framework. Because the mapping is maintained as data, both are content updates rather than rebuilds. The same commitment covers a bill's failure: within ten days the portal records the event here, relabels the affected framework, and, if that framework was the default, moves the default to the operative administrative framework.

- September 15, 2026: the Senate rejected cloture on the merged market-structure text, 49 to 50. Recorded September 26, 2026.
